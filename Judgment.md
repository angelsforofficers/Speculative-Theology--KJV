Under the New Covenant, God explicitly declares regarding believers' sins and iniquities that He will remember them no more. In biblical usage, when God "remembers not," He refuses to bring the matter up for legal review or hold it against the person. If God has declared the record closed and forgotten, He will not replay it in heaven.

The Record Was Blotted Out at the Cross (Colossians 2:13–14; Isaiah 43:25): Paul explains that God forgave all trespasses by blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us, taking it out of the way, and nailing it to the cross. A document that has been blotted out and nailed to the cross no longer exists to be read aloud at a future tribunal.

Total Removal of Guilt (Psalm 103:12; Micah 7:19): Scripture describes forgiven sins as removed as far as the east is from the west and cast into the depths of the sea.

Freedom from Condemnation (John 5:24; Romans 8:1): Christ stated plainly that the one who hears His word and believes on Him that sent Him has everlasting life, shall not come into condemnation, and is already passed from death unto life. Calling a believer forward to answer for forgiven moral failures would be a form of double jeopardy—demanding a second accounting for a debt Christ already paid in full.

Rather than putting a believer's failures on display for embarrassment, every man's work is tried by fire to see "of what sort it is." Wood, hay, and stubble are simply burned up and turned to ashes. Everything worthless is permanently consumed so that only what is pure and eternal remains.

If their works burn up, they "suffer loss" (the forfeiture of rewards and crowns they could have earned), yet the person themselves is saved, "yet so as by fire." Furthermore, 1 Corinthians 4:5 notes that when the Lord brings to light the hidden things and manifests the counsels (motives) of the hearts, the ultimate result is that "then shall every man have praise of God."

The most logical and scripturally consistent conclusion is that the Judgment Seat of Christ is an awards and stewardship evaluation, not a criminal trial or a public shaming session. A believer's sins were judged at the cross and blotted out forever; a believer's works and motives as a servant will be tested by fire at the Judgment Seat, burning away everything done in the flesh and leaving behind only what is worthy of eternal reward.

\[11\] And I saw a great white throne, and him that sat on it, from whose face the earth and the heaven fled away; and there was found no place for them.  
\[12\] And I saw the dead, small and great, stand before God; and the books were opened: and another book was opened, which is the book of life: and the dead were judged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their works.  
\[13\] And the sea gave up the dead which were in it; and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them: and they were judged every man according to their works.

- It's the dead that are resurrected to judgment. As a saint, you've already got a seat at the table.

**Ecclesiastes 12:7**  
“Then shall the dust return to the earth as it was: and the spirit shall return unto God who gave it.”

- Saved body soul spirit \- resulting 3 part harmony  
- Unsaved body soul spirit \- 2 parts in the lake of fire. That's why the river of life burns. There's a piece eternally missing \- by that souls choice

**Deuteronomy 9:21**  
“And I took your sin, the calf which ye had made, and burnt it with fire, and stamped it, and ground it very small, even until it was as small as dust: and I cast the dust thereof into the brook that descended out of the mount.”

- Type of the judgement  
- Took your sin  
- The calf which ye had made \- the works of your hands  
- Tried by fire and he takes the residue, stamps it and grinds it very small (as dust) and casts it into the river of life that proceeds from the throne, the same which might pour into the lake of fire to the lost.  
- The river of life from the throne being a type of the river that washed away the blood from the temple sacrifices.
